photon_canon.contrib.diffusion_theory.create_integrated_diffusion_approximation(r_range, alpha_i=0, n_rel=1.33, g=0.9)

Build a callable that returns the radially integrated diffuse reflectance

\[\tilde{R}_d(\mu_s, \mu_a) \;=\; 2\pi \int_{r_{\min}}^{r_{\max}} r \, R_d\!\bigl(r;\mu_s,\mu_a\bigr)\;dr,\]

where \(R_d(r;\mu_s,\mu_a)\) is the spatially resolved reflectance given by diffusion_approximation().

Parameters

r_range(float, float)

Integration bounds \((r_{\min}, r_{\max})\) in millimetres.

alpha_ifloat, optional

Angle of incidence of the illumination in radians (0 ⇒ normal incidence). Default 0.

n_relfloat, optional

Relative refractive index \(n_2/n_1\) (tissue / external medium). Default 1.33.

gfloat, optional

Scattering anisotropy factor. Default 0.9.

Returns

Callable[[float, float], float]

A function f(mu_s, mu_a) that evaluates \(\tilde{R}_d(\mu_s,\mu_a)\) using scipy.integrate.quad().

Notes

The integrand is

\[f(r,\mu_s,\mu_a) \;=\; 2\pi\,r\,R_d(r;\mu_s,\mu_a),\]

so the returned function effectively computes

\[\tilde{R}_d(\mu_s,\mu_a) \;=\; \int_{r_{\min}}^{r_{\max}} f(r,\mu_s,\mu_a)\;dr.\]

The numerical integration is performed with adaptive quadrature (scipy.integrate.quad()).

See Also

diffusion_approximation : Point-wise diffusion reflectance model.

Examples

>>> integ_Rd = create_integrated_diffusion_approximation((0.0, 5.0))
>>> integ_Rd(mu_s=10.0, mu_a=0.1)
0.0487

photon_canon.contrib.diffusion_theory.diffusion_approximation(mu_s, mu_a, r=1, alpha_i=0, n_rel=1.33, g=0.9)

Calculate the steady-state diffuse reflectance \(R_d(r)\) predicted by the diffusion approximation for a semi-infinite, homogeneous medium illuminated at its surface.

The formulation follows Farrell et al. (1992) and accounts for oblique illumination by adjusting the effective source depth and radial offset.

Parameters:

  • mu_s (float) – Scattering coefficient \(\mu_s\;[\text{mm}^{-1}]\).

  • mu_a (float) – Absorption coefficient \(\mu_a\;[\text{mm}^{-1}]\).

  • r (float, optional) – Radial distance on the surface where \(R_d\) is evaluated (in mm). Default is 1.

  • alpha_i (float, optional) – Angle of incidence of the source in radians (0 = normal incidence). Default is 0.

  • n_rel (float, optional) – Relative refractive index \(n_2/n_1\) (tissue / external medium). Default is 1.33.

  • g (float, optional) – Scattering anisotropy factor. Default is 0.9.

Returns:

Diffuse reflectance \(R_d(r)\).

Return type:

float

Notes

The diffusion approximation is valid when \(\mu_s' \gg \mu_a\), where the reduced scattering coefficient is \(\mu_s' = (1-g)\,\mu_s\). Key intermediate quantities are

\[\begin{split}\mu_t' &= \mu_s' + \mu_a \\ l' &= \frac{1}{\mu_t'} \\ a' &= \frac{\mu_s'}{\mu_t'} \\[6pt] D &= \frac{1}{3\left(\mu_s' + \beta\mu_a\right)}, \qquad \beta = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{normal incidence} \\ 0.35 & \text{oblique incidence} \end{cases} \\[6pt] \mu_{\mathrm{eff}} &= \sqrt{\frac{\mu_a}{D}}\end{split}\]

The diffuse reflectance is

\[R_d(r) \;=\; \bigl(1-R_{\mathrm{sp}}\bigr) \Bigl[ a' z' \, \frac{\bigl(1+\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\rho_1\bigr) e^{-\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\rho_1}}{4\pi\rho_1^{3}} \;+\; a'\!\bigl(z' + 4D\bigr) \frac{\bigl(1+\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\rho_2\bigr) e^{-\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}\rho_2}}{4\pi\rho_2^{3}} \Bigr],\]

where \(\rho_1\) and \(\rho_2\) are the distances from the observation point to the real and image sources, respectively, and \(R_{\mathrm{sp}}\) is the specular Fresnel reflectance.

References

[1]

M. W. Farrell, M. S. Patterson, B. C. Wilson A diffusion-theory model of spatially resolved, steady-state diffuse reflectance for the noninvasive determination of tissue optical properties in vivo. Medical Physics 19 (1992) 879–888.

[2]

S. L. Jacques Optical properties of biological tissues: a review. Physics in Medicine & Biology 58 (2013) R37–R61.
